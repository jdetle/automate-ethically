## TL;DR
- decision: proceed — the ladder and toolkit ship, with two required fixes before merge
- key reason: the mechanism (mailto: instead of a working Action Network integration) is honest about its own limits everywhere it appears, but the six-rung ladder currently asks a first-timer to sort itself, which is exactly the cognitive-load failure this review exists to catch
- top unresolved risk: the mailto: links are untested against real email clients on mobile, and nobody has verified what a filled `body=` param actually renders like in Gmail/Apple Mail
- immediate next step: add a single default recommended action above the ladder; ship a `<meta>` that keeps mailto links from silently truncating

## Context pack
- Proposal: ship `/act` (a six-rung "action ladder") and `/toolkit` (one-pager, council script, find-your-council guide) as the site's first real organizing infrastructure, replacing a single mailto CTA. Audience of record: "an everyday citizen who has never contacted a local government body before" (per the prior 2026-08-29 citizen-appeal-urgency review, which named this exact audience).
- Constraint: Action Network is not yet integrated (no account exists). Every AN-dependent rung (join, petition, write, become-a-lead) currently routes through a `mailto:` link with a prefilled subject/body, per `src/pages/act.astro:9-16` (the file's own TODO comment names this explicitly and commits to swapping the hrefs once AN exists).
- Constraint: no fundraising language, no implying the PAC exists (README content rules) — not contested in this review, checked for completeness only.
- Not decided by this review (Juno's boundary, stated up front): whether Action Network is the right eventual mechanism (already decided in a prior planning pass), the visual design system, or the evidence/citation policy (FACTS.md — unchanged here).

## Phase 1 — Offense

**Maren (shipper).** Veto condition: I will veto if this ships without a single, unambiguous "start here" — six co-equal options with no default is a shipped decision paralysis, not a shipped feature. Claim she expects agreement on: the ladder's actual bottleneck isn't the AN gap, it's rung 4 (`src/pages/act.astro:96-118`, "Write to your officials") — it demands the visitor read a paragraph, understand a split between state and city mechanisms, copy a block of legal-register text ("consequential decision," "HB 178"), and find their own representative's contact page, all inside a rung nominally costed at "10 minutes." That estimate is false advertising and she'll hold the room to it. Claim she expects rejection on: rungs 1 and 2 (join, petition) should be merged into one action, not two — a first-time visitor does not have a mental model that distinguishes "get on a mailing list" from "sign a petition," and asking for two separate emails with two different subject lines (`act.astro:29-34`, `act.astro:46-51`) is two units of friction sold as one rung each. Worst case: a visitor reads six rungs, correctly notices #4 is harder than advertised, and disengages from the whole page rather than doing #1.

**Iris (auditor).** Veto condition — **fired:** no rung may claim a time cost the markup itself contradicts. `act.astro:96` labels rung 4 "10 minutes." The rung's own content requires: reading a two-sentence disambiguation, reading a four-sentence legal-register letter template, navigating away to find a representative's contact page (external, unverified, no link provided), and pasting the text in. She has not run this end to end but the artifact-count alone (one page navigation this rung sends the user to sites we don't control, one copy-paste, one composition on an unfamiliar site) exceeds what "10 minutes" declares for a first-timer, as opposed to someone who has done this before. She distinguishes: the *council* letter template correctly declines to give a false time estimate implicitly (it just says "here's how to find your council" and points to rung 5's dedicated toolkit page) — rung 4's state-legislator half does not get the same honesty. Second condition: the QR code on the one-pager (`toolkit/one-pager.astro:12-17`) encodes `https://automate-ethically.com/act?source=one-pager-qr` — she has not verified this URL 200s in production (the site's canonical domain has an open apex-binding issue per README's Deploy section), so a printed, physically-distributed artifact could point at a broken link with no way to correct it once printed copies exist in the world.

**Kai (explorer).** Attacks the premise: the screen argues "pick your rung, all six are real options" (`act.astro:20-23`, "you don't have to do all six of these — even one moves the needle") — but four of six rungs quietly resolve to the exact same mechanism: composing an email to `join@automate-ethically.com` with a different subject line (`act.astro:29-34`, `46-51`, `181-186`). The visitor is shown six doors; four of them open into the same room. That's not dishonest exactly — the page never claims the mechanisms differ — but it is a UI that promises more infrastructure than exists, and a user who notices (opens their email client four times, sees the same "To:" address each time) will correctly conclude the whole ladder is one guy's inbox with extra steps. Claim he expects agreement on: rung 3 ("Share it") is the only rung that is genuinely low-friction *and* mechanically distinct from the others — printing a page and forwarding an email are real, different actions a phone-only user can do without composing prose. Claim he expects rejection on: the find-your-council guide (`content/toolkit/find-your-council.md`) is written entirely in second person, present tense, warm — and then rung 4's letter template on `/act` switches into "I'm a constituent... requiring a named, accountable human decision-maker" register, which is not how the rest of the site talks to this exact audience. A first-timer who was gaining confidence through "Step 1, Step 2, Step 3" suddenly gets handed policy-memo language to paste as if it were their own words. Second-order effect: the visitor either sends language that isn't theirs (learned ventriloquism, not organizing) or bounces off the rung entirely.

**Rafe (steward).** Every rung that routes to `mailto:${email}` is a doc masquerading as a feature: five hardcoded subject/body strings (`act.astro:29-34`, `46-51`, `76-79`, `181-186`) that must all be found and swapped the day Action Network exists, per the file's own TODO — that's four to six edit sites for one migration, with nothing enforcing that all get updated together (a partial swap would leave, say, the petition on AN but the local-lead form still on mailto, silently). His artefact: the TODO comment at `act.astro:9-16` names the intent correctly but there is no single source of truth for "which rungs are AN-backed today" — it's implicit in which `href`s happen to start with `mailto:`. Condition: before this ships as anything other than a stopgap, the five mailto hrefs should read from one named constant/config object, not five inline `encodeURIComponent` calls, so the eventual AN migration is a data change, not a five-site find-and-replace under time pressure. Second flag: `content.config.ts`'s `updated` date and the toolkit docs' own "Last checked" line (rendered by `ToolkitDoc.astro:15-19`) currently just mirror the frontmatter — nothing ties them to FACTS.md's 6-month recheck discipline the way the index page's citations do. A council script or one-pager that goes stale (Montana HB 178 gets amended, say) has no recheck trigger the way a FACTS.md-registered number does.

**Juno (mediator).** Decision boundary: this review decides whether the *shape and honesty* of the action ladder and toolkit serve a first-time, non-technical visitor — not whether Action Network is the right eventual backend (decided already), not the visual design system, not the citation/evidence policy. Scope-creep risk in the proposal itself: "six rungs" was sized to match a previously-planned ladder design, not validated against this specific audience — nothing in the artifacts tested whether six is the right number for someone who has never done any of this, versus, say, three.

## Phase 2 — Defense

**Maren.** Steelman of Iris: the strongest version of her veto is that a false time estimate is worse than no time estimate — it spends trust the page can't get back, and trust is the entire product for a site with no other credibility signal (no traffic, no press, no existing member base). Accepted, and she converts on the spot: rung 4's "10 minutes" splits into two honest numbers or drops the number for the city-council half, in the same commit that ships this page, not a follow-up. She answers Kai's "four doors, one room" claim directly: agrees it's true and defends it anyway — a stopgap that is honest about being a stopgap (the page never claims six distinct mechanisms) ships faster than a stopgap disguised as finished infrastructure, and speed matters inside the 2026 legislative-session window the prior review already established. Residual risk she accepts by name: shipping mailto-backed rungs before AN exists means four of six rungs are less durable and less trackable (no signup count, no petition tally) than the finished version will be — accepted because the alternative is shipping nothing until AN integration lands.

**Iris.** Steelman of Maren's rejection-claim (merge rungs 1+2): the strongest version is that distinguishing "join a list" from "sign a petition" is a distinction the *organization* cares about (list-building vs. demonstrating support) that the *visitor* has no reason to hold in their head — two mental categories for one perceived action is exactly the kind of unforced complexity her veto exists to catch. She partially accepts: this doesn't discharge her fired veto (the time-cost claim is a markup-verifiable fact, not a UX judgment call), but she agrees it's a second, related defect. She does not discharge her veto on Maren's word alone — discharge requires the "10 minutes" claim be corrected in the shipped markup, which has not happened yet in this transcript. On Rafe's QR/domain point: she adds a condition rather than a separate veto — a printed artifact encoding a URL is a one-way door (unlike a webpage, it cannot be silently fixed later), so the apex-domain binding (README: "the apex binding is disabled in ACA") must be resolved, or the QR must point at `www.automate-ethically.com` (the binding that currently works), before any physical copies are printed and distributed.

**Kai.** Steelman of Rafe: the strongest version of the doc-not-feature claim is that a hardcoded mailto is worse than it looks precisely because it *works* today — there's no error, no broken build, nothing forcing the migration to happen, so "swap five hrefs later" quietly becomes "never," the same way TODO comments rot in every codebase. Accepted fully — he's not defending the mailto mechanism, he's defending the choice to ship it *labeled* as temporary, which the page does. He answers Maren's rejection-claim on rung 4's register shift: agrees it's real, holds it as dissent rather than a blocker — the site's own prior review (2026-08-29) already established the mailto pattern as an acceptable v1 stopgap for a harder problem (no backend at all); this is the same shape of compromise at a different rung, and he won't re-litigate a precedent this site already set for itself. He does concede the register mismatch is worse here than in the original mailto case, because rung 4 asks the visitor to *impersonate their own voice* with borrowed language, which the original "one email" CTA never did.

**Rafe.** Steelman of Kai's premise attack: the strongest version is that a page which *looks* like six pieces of infrastructure but *is* one inbox creates a specific, nameable future cost — the day AN integration lands, whoever does it has no artifact distinguishing "this rung already works, don't touch it" from "this rung is still the stopgap," other than manually re-reading every href. Accepted as a real gap, not just a style complaint. He attaches himself as owner: before or alongside the AN migration, the five mailto call sites collapse into one lookup keyed by rung id, so "is this rung AN-backed yet" becomes a one-line answer instead of a grep. Residual risk he accepts by name: shipping without that refactor today, on the grounds that introducing config-driven indirection for content that's about to change again (once AN exists) is itself a small maintenance bet — he'd rather take the risk of a slightly-messy stopgap than build abstraction for a shape that isn't final yet.

**Juno.** Steelman of the proposal itself: the strongest version of "ship the six-rung ladder now" is that the alternative — waiting for Action Network before shipping any action page at all — directly reopens Kai's dissent from the prior review (the mailto-only site "teaches learned helplessness"), and that dissent's stated closing condition ("a take-action page ships with at least one lower-friction action") is unconditionally met by rungs 3 and 5 regardless of what happens to rungs 1, 2, 4, and 6. Accepted as the reshaping constraint: this review should not block on the AN gap, because closing Kai's prior dissent does not require AN to exist.

## Phase 3 — Synthesis

**Merged proposal — "honest ladder, corrected costs" (single option):**

1. **Fix the time-cost claim on rung 4** before merge: split "Write to your officials" into two honestly-labeled halves — a real, numbered time estimate for the state-legislator letter (the part with a template ready to paste), and no time estimate (or a distinctly higher one, e.g. "15–20 minutes, first time") for the city-council half, which requires external navigation this site doesn't control.
2. **Add one visually distinct default recommendation** above the six rungs — not a seventh rung, a single sentence: "New here? Start with rung 1 — it takes thirty seconds." This directly answers Maren's paralysis veto without removing any of the six options Juno's boundary preserves.
3. **Domain/QR gate:** the one-pager's QR code must resolve against a working canonical host before any copy is printed for physical distribution — either the apex binding gets fixed, or the QR (and the on-page URL text beside it) point at `www.automate-ethically.com` in the interim. This is a print-before-ship gate, not a code-review gate: the toolkit page can stay live with the current QR while the domain issue is open, but nothing gets run through a physical printer until it's resolved.
4. **Rung 4's letter template register**: recorded as Kai's dissent (below), not merged into this proposal — a register rewrite is a copy task, and the site's DRAFT-marker convention already exists for exactly this; add the marker rather than hand-rewriting inside this review.
5. **Deferred, not blocking:** collapsing the five mailto call sites into one config (Rafe) and merging rungs 1+2 (Maren's rejection-claim, partially accepted by Iris) are both named as follow-ups, not merge gates — neither is a claim about what ships today being *wrong*, both are claims about what ships today being *improvable*.

**Tradeoff table:**

| Choice | Gain | Cost | Carrier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Correct rung 4's time estimate now | closes Iris's fired veto, restores trust budget | none — this is a copy fix, not a scope change | Iris |
| Ship six rungs (not fewer) today | preserves the full ladder design, no re-litigation of prior scoping | first-timer decision paralysis until the "start here" fix lands | Maren |

| Leave rung 4's borrowed-voice register unfixed | ships today, doesn't block on a copy rewrite | visitor may paste language that isn't their own, or bounce off the rung | Kai (dissent, below) |
| Defer the mailto-consolidation refactor | avoids building abstraction for a shape that's about to change | manual grep is the only way to know which rungs are still stopgap once AN lands | Rafe |

**Veto status:** Iris vetoed on the unsupported "10 minutes" claim on rung 4 — **resolved.** `src/pages/act.astro` now splits rung 4 into a "state representative — about 10 minutes" half and a "city council — 15 to 20 minutes, longer the first time" half, with no single false estimate covering both. Per her own rule, this is recorded as discharged because the correction landed in the shipped markup, not on another persona's word; it re-fires automatically if a future edit collapses the two estimates back into one.

**Post-review fixes applied (same session):** item 2 (a "New here? Start with rung 1" note above the ladder) also shipped, addressing Maren's paralysis veto condition. Item 4 (Kai's dissent) got a partial fix — the letter template's opening line moved from "I'm a constituent... requiring a named, accountable human decision-maker" to "I'm a neighbor... a named person has to review it and be accountable" — plus an explicit `<!-- DRAFT: rewrite -->` marker citing this review, since a full register pass is a copy task for John, not something to hand-finish inside a review transcript. Kai's dissent therefore stays open (recorded below) pending that pass, but the worst of the policy-memo register is gone.

**Dissent (Kai, minority):** rung 4's letter template asks a first-timer to paste policy-register language ("consequential decision," citing "HB 178") as if it were their own voice, breaking with every other rung's plain, second-person register (`find-your-council.md`, `council-script.md`'s explicit "cross out anything that doesn't sound like you"). Trigger condition to close: the letter template gets the same "make it yours" framing the council script already has, or gets rewritten in plain language to match. Stays open across reviews until met.

**Kill criterion:** if, after this ships, more than one visitor is observed replying to the mailto confirming they didn't understand which rung to start with (via any channel — email replies, direct feedback), the "start here" fix (item 2) is insufficient and the ladder should collapse to three rungs, not six. (No analytics exist on click-through by rung today — this criterion binds to direct signal, consistent with the site's standing no-analytics-by-default posture on this class of question.)

**Claim that most reshaped the proposal (Juno):** Iris's distinction between "the mailto mechanism is an accepted stopgap" (already settled by the prior review) and "the mailto mechanism made a false claim about the effort involved" (not previously reviewed, and false regardless of what backend eventually replaces it) — this separated a legitimate, already-adjudicated architecture compromise from a genuine, fixable copy defect that had been riding along inside it unexamined.

**Residual risk I am accepting (Juno):** shipping today with rungs 1, 2, 4 (partially), and 6 still resolving to one person's inbox, with no tracking of how many people actually used any of them, and no consolidated config making that stopgap state legible to whoever does the AN migration later. I accept this because the alternative — delaying the whole ladder until Action Network exists — reopens a dissent this site already closed once, inside a legislative window the site's own evidence section says is active now.

**Vote:** 4 proceed, 1 proceed-with-veto-outstanding (Iris — proceed once the rung-4 time-cost correction lands, which the synthesis treats as a merge gate, not a follow-up). Kai records dissent on rung 4's register, separate from his vote to proceed. Not unanimous; no low-signal flag.

## Core Takeaways
- The mailto-as-stopgap pattern this site already adopted for its original CTA is being reused correctly in spirit (labeled, TODO'd, honest about being temporary) but was extended to a rung whose *content* — not its mechanism — made a claim the mechanism can't support ("10 minutes" for a task requiring off-site navigation).
- A ladder with no default rung is read by a first-timer as a test, not an invitation; the fix is one sentence, not a redesign.
- Printed, physically-distributed artifacts (the one-pager's QR) are a one-way door in a way a webpage isn't — they need a stricter pre-flight check (a working canonical domain) than anything else on the site currently gets.

## Decision Memo
- recommended option: "honest ladder, corrected costs" (single merged option above)
- unresolved risks: Iris's veto (rung 4 time-cost claim) outstanding until the markup changes; Kai's dissent (rung 4 register) open across reviews
- kill criterion: more than one visitor signals confusion about where to start → collapse six rungs to three
- rollout gates: rung 4 time-cost split lands in the same commit as anything else from this review; QR/domain check happens before any physical printing, not before web publish
- rollback trigger: same as kill criterion
- residual risk accepted by: Juno (four of six rungs remain untracked, unconsolidated mailto stopgaps); Rafe (deferred mailto-consolidation refactor)
